## PHASE_A5_BASELINE_FROZEN.md

Reports 3-seed result: Dice 0.9100 ± 0.0005 (seeds: 0.9107, 0.9095, 0.9097), statistically indistinguishable from a retired single-head baseline (90.92% ± 0.05%). Cross-reference: explicitly supersedes/retires the "single-head baseline" (archived, not a separate phase doc per se, but flagged as a superseded prior result). No hedging on its own frozen-baseline verdict.

## PHASE_C0_WEIGHT_ABLATION_RESULTS.md

Tests two competing hypotheses (H1: gradient conflict genuinely resolved vs H2: evidential loss drowned out by fixed 0.5/0.5 weighting) via three fixed loss-weight configs (0.8/0.2, 0.5/0.5, 0.2/0.8 focal/evidential). Explicit result: "neither H1 nor H2 as stated" — the trunk gradient-magnitude ratio (~0.09-0.10) and cosine-conflict resolution timeline are statistically invariant across an 8x swing in evidential weight, falsifying H2's literal claim that reweighting could fix the imbalance. Dice is flat (0.8787-0.8797) across configs but ECE (calibration) improves monotonically with evidential weight (0.1152→0.1067→0.1018). Frames this as motivating a dynamic controller (ABO) that acts directly on gradients rather than the loss scalar, since static reweighting structurally cannot move the trunk ratio. No self-contradiction — the doc's own headline ("neither hypothesis... a third finding") is consistent throughout the body. No bug-fix mention. Cross-reference: builds directly on "Phase B" (gradient-conflict/ratio findings) and motivates Phase C's ABO design — explicitly said to strengthen the case beyond what either original hypothesis would have given.

## PHASE_D_ANALYSIS_REPORT.md

Definitive 50-epoch × 3-seed ABO-vs-baseline comparison. Headline: Dice/IoU/F1 are statistically indistinguishable between ABO (0.9100±0.0006) and baseline (0.9100±0.0007) — a clean null result — despite ABO robustly doubling the effective trunk gradient ratio (0.1886±0.0007 vs baseline's ~0.098), confirming ABO worked mechanically but the causal chain into Dice breaks at the last link. This document is notable for actively catching and correcting a misleading correlation within its own analysis (the r=-0.88 pooled figure) rather than an external contradiction — a caught statistical-confound "bug" in interpretation, not code. Cross-reference: explicitly builds on/supersedes-in-part the working hypothesis going into "Phase C" (gradient magnitude imbalance as the Dice bottleneck), which it says is "falsified... for this architecture and dataset," and points forward to `abo_frozen_lessons_learned` memory / a forthcoming candidate-optimizer-concepts document (i.e., PHASE_E3).

## PHASE_E1_LITERATURE_MATRIX.md

45-paper literature survey (6 categories, 2023-2026) mapping recent multi-objective-optimization / gradient-manipulation / medical-segmentation / evidential-learning / representation-learning literature against this project's own four measured findings (transient gradient conflict; static reweighting can't move the trunk ratio; ABO can; moving the ratio doesn't move Dice but does move ECE). No self-contradiction; this is a synthesis document, not a results claim. No bug-fix mention. ## PHASE_E1_1_BOUNDARY_VS_EVIDENCE.md

Tests whether the evidential branch is merely a boundary-distance detector (H0) vs encoding something richer (H1), using 60,000 voxels sampled from 30 validation volumes (no new inference). Result: H0 rejected — boundary distance explains at most R²=0.150 (degree-4 polynomial) of evidence variance, in the "barely explains it" pre-registered band. The most informative finding: incorrect voxels have dramatically lower evidence than correct voxels (mean 6.3 vs 19.8, Welch t=10.62, p=3.4e-17) despite occupying nearly the same narrow boundary-distance band, ruling out boundary distance as the driver of this separation. Also finds class-conditional Pearson correlations with opposite signs (tumor +0.353 vs background -0.160), ruling out a single global boundary-based model. Verdict (H0 rejected, H1 supported) is consistent throughout, not walked back. No bug-fix mention. Cross-reference: explicitly motivates continuing into E1.2, and references Concept 0 in PHASE_E3_CANDIDATE_CONCEPTS.md as the eventual next step.

## PHASE_E1_2_FEATURE_NORM_ANALYSIS.md

Tests whether evidence ≈ f(feature-norm ‖z‖) (H0), using the same 60,000-voxel dataset as E1.1. Headline pooled polynomial R²=0.572 looks like a large effect at first, but the document explicitly flags this as "misleading in isolation" and walks through why: a Simpson's-paradox-style class confound, where tumor voxels (r=+0.907) and background voxels (r=-0.680) have strong, oppositely-signed, largely non-overlapping relationships that a pooled polynomial fit conflates into a spurious U-shaped curve — this is the file's own text actively correcting a naive reading of its own headline number, not an external contradiction. Once boundary distance is controlled for, feature norm's independent linear contribution is small (+0.043 incremental R², boundary+featnorm together only R²=0.086), and feature norm separates correct/incorrect voxels less than half as strongly as evidence does (Cohen's d 1.29 vs. 3.09 from E1.1). Final verdict: "H0 is rejected" but explicitly framed as an "in-between" result, not a clean rejection — genuinely hedged in its own interpretation section, stating the result lands in neither of the two clean pre-registered decision-tree boxes. No bug-fix mention. Cross-reference: explicitly builds on PHASE_E1_1_BOUNDARY_VS_EVIDENCE.md ("the prior filter this builds on") and forwards to Concept 0/1 in PHASE_E3_CANDIDATE_CONCEPTS.md, noting representation diagnostics should account for the newly found class asymmetry.

## PHASE_E1_3_LATENT_GEOMETRY.md

Redirected from E1.2's finding of opposite-signed class relationships, this asks whether tumor/background form separate clusters in the 32-dim `dec1` latent space with uncertainty concentrated at the transition zone. Result: near-perfect linear separability (AUC=0.9995); background forms a tight cluster (mean dist-to-centroid 4.35) while tumor is ~3x more diffuse (13.43); evidence forms a visible continuous gradient rising from the tumor/background transition zone (low) to the tumor cluster's tip (high); latent boundary distance explains more of evidence's variance (R²=0.219) than image-space boundary distance did in E1.1 (R²=0.150); incorrect voxels concentrate almost bimodally near the latent boundary (Cohen's d=1.41, mean |dist| 0.648 vs 1.456 correct). Notably, the document itself proactively catches and defuses a potential self-contradiction: a raw AUC horse-race making it look like image boundary distance (AUC 0.995) "beats" evidence (0.965) and latent boundary distance (0.776) at separating correct/incorrect voxels, which it explicitly argues is a near-tautology of segmentation-error geography, not evidence that boundary distance explains evidence *magnitude* — carefully distinguishing this from E1.1's still-valid claim. No bug-fix mention. Cross-reference: explicitly builds on and extends PHASE_E1_1 and PHASE_E1_2, and sharpens PHASE_E3_CANDIDATE_CONCEPTS.md's Concept 0/1 into a more specific target (decision-boundary margin/shape, not generic entanglement).

## PHASE_E1_4_DENSITY_ANALYSIS.md

Tests whether local manifold density (mean distance to 20 nearest neighbors in `dec1` space) explains evidence variance beyond E1.1's and E1.3's boundary-distance measures. Finds density is a strong, genuinely independent signal: incorrect voxels sit in regions ~12x sparser than correct voxels (mean ρ 2.660 vs 0.229, Cohen's d=-3.005, nearly matching evidence's own d=3.09), and density is essentially uncorrelated with latent boundary distance (r=-0.0068, p=0.097, not significant) — ruling out density as a repackaging of the E1.3 finding. Combined regression (image boundary + latent boundary + density) reaches R²=0.140 vs 0.097 without density, an incremental +0.043. Self-classifies its own result against pre-registered thresholds as "Case B, bordering on Case C" — explicitly flags that the R²-based framing (moderate, Case B) undersells the effect-size evidence (Cohen's d nearly as large as evidence's own separation), an internal nuance/hedge rather than a contradiction. No bug-fix mention. Cross-reference: explicitly extends PHASE_E1_3_LATENT_GEOMETRY.md and refines the algorithm-design recommendation in PHASE_E2_LITERATURE_REVIEW.md (argues density should be a second explicit input alongside boundary-distance margin loss).

## PHASE_E2_LITERATURE_REVIEW.md

Pre-implementation ~30-paper literature review checking whether "margin/boundary-geometry regularization on latent embeddings" is already standard practice before building any of 5 proposed LBGO variants. Verdict: the general strategy (margin/triplet loss on pixel embeddings, prototype-distance segmentation, distance-to-cluster-as-confidence) is well-precedented individually, but no single paper combines an evidential/Beta uncertainty head with a margin loss on trunk embeddings validated by a latent-vs-image boundary R² comparison — that specific combination is judged genuinely novel. Recommends the "confidence/evidence-weighted margin pressure using the model's own evidential-head output" variant as the headline contribution (strongest novelty), with plain margin loss and hard boundary mining relegated to baseline/ablation status since those have strong direct precedent (Pixel-wise Triplet Learning KBS 2022, Distribution-Aware Margin Calibration IJCV 2022). No self-contradiction; verdict is nuanced from the start ("well-trodden... but narrower combination is genuinely novel") rather than walked back later. No bug-fix mention. Cross-reference: built on PHASE_E1_3_LATENT_GEOMETRY.md's diagnostic finding, and explicitly sharpens PHASE_E3_CANDIDATE_CONCEPTS.md's earlier Concept 0-3.

## PHASE_E5_ALGORITHM_DESIGN.md

Full pre-implementation math specification for "Evidence-Guided Geometry Optimization" (EGGO): a two-term loss (separation + local-density compactification) gated multiplicatively by evidential-head uncertainty and weighted by boundary-proximity/density terms, each hyperparameter (τ_b≈0.972, τ_ρ≈0.781) explicitly derived from measured correct/incorrect crossover points in E1.3/E1.4 data, mirroring how ABO's r_target was derived. Contains an explicit honesty table (§4.4) distinguishing borrowed math (hinge/margin skeletons from De Brabandere et al. 2017 and Wen et al. 2016 center loss; uncertainty-gating mechanism from DyCON) from genuinely novel pieces (gating by an evidential/Beta head specifically; local rather than global density-weighted centroid). Also runs a complexity analysis showing dense per-voxel computation is infeasible (~10^14 FLOPs/batch) and specifies required mitigations (voxel subsampling, stratified uncertainty-weighted sampling, in-batch-only pos/neg sets, periodic boundary-classifier refresh). Lists 6 explicit failure modes including "gate degeneracy," "noise-driven false sparsity" (explicitly flagged as "not yet checked against data"), and "boundary classifier staleness" — a self-identified risk later directly addressed/corrected in PHASE_E9. Status explicitly "Draft... not yet reviewed, not yet implemented" — no premature verdict to contradict. Cross-reference: builds on PHASE_E5_LOSS_MATH_LITERATURE.md, PHASE_E1_3/E1_4, and PHASE_E2; is itself later stress-tested in PHASE_E6 and partially superseded in mechanism-choice by PHASE_E7/E8 (density term dropped) and corrected in attachment design by PHASE_E9 (see below).

## PHASE_E6_STRESS_TEST.md

Actively attempts to falsify the EGGO design (no training, existing data only) before any implementation. Key finding: in a full joint regression (all 4 predictors simultaneously, unlike E1.4's pairwise partial correlations), density's standardized beta for predicting evidence drops to rank 3/4 (β=-0.081) — explicitly called "an important, honest downgrade from E1.4's headline framing," a direct self-correction of an earlier document's own emphasis. Also finds the density effect is stable across a 16x range of neighbor-count k (Cohen's d varies only ~2%), reassuring on robustness. A four-group breakdown initially used a flawed median-split methodology that the document explicitly flags as "a methodological error, not a finding" (put zero incorrect voxels in the "dense" bucket by construction) before redoing it correctly with the E1.4-derived crossover threshold — an explicit caught-and-fixed methodological bug. The corrected breakdown finds density has high sensitivity (95.3% of incorrect voxels are sparse) but very high false-positive rate (96% of sparse voxels are actually correct) — used to justify (not undermine) the design's existing uncertainty-gating of density. States Hypothesis D (circularity — does the evidential loss itself produce the sparsity pattern?) is "not addressed by this analysis" and remains open. Overall verdict: "the algorithm survives... with real, honest downgrades to its confidence level, not a clean pass" — an explicitly hedged, non-triumphant verdict. Cross-reference: stress-tests PHASE_E5_ALGORITHM_DESIGN.md directly; its findings are carried forward into PHASE_E7, E8 (density term dropped as a direct consequence).

## PHASE_E7_CAUSALITY_TEST.md

A full 50-epoch observational training run (seed 0, no EGGO changes) with diagnostics re-run at 7 checkpoints, testing Hypothesis D (does geometry precede or follow calibration) — final epoch-50 Dice (0.9107) exactly matches the frozen baseline, confirming no confound from the added checkpoint logic. Finding 1 (clearest): latent boundary AUC saturates almost instantly (0.9945 at epoch 1) while ECE improves gradually over all 50 epochs (0.364→0.046) — real evidence that class-separability geometry precedes calibration, not the reverse. Finding 2 (density) is explicitly "more equivocal" — present early (Cohen's d=-1.150 at epoch 1) but continues strengthening on a timescale that tracks calibration rather than clearly preceding it, and the document is explicit that it "cannot cleanly separate" two competing explanations here. Finding 3 explicitly reports a null/noisy result (boundary→evidence R² bounces non-monotonically, 0.208→0.059→...→0.296) and states plainly "this finding should not be used to support or refute the causality question" rather than omitting or spinning it. Overall verdict labeled "partial, honest answer... not the clean... result that would maximally justify EGGO, but also not the null result that would kill it" — explicitly hedged. Recommends proceeding with margin-only EGGO (density dropped), consistent with E6. No bug-fix mention beyond methodology honesty. Cross-reference: directly responds to Hypothesis D raised in PHASE_E6_STRESS_TEST.md; feeds into PHASE_E5's design and the simplification made in PHASE_E8.

## PHASE_E8_EGGO_V1_SIMPLIFIED.md

Formally simplifies full EGGO down to a single-mechanism "EGGO-v1": keeps the uncertainty gate and boundary-proximity margin/separation loss, explicitly removes the density weight, compactification loss, k-NN computation, and local centroid — citing E6 (density's modest/rank-3-4 independent contribution) and E7 (density's ambiguous, calibration-tracking temporal ordering) as the two converging reasons. Explicitly frames this as avoiding "the earlier mistake pattern" of adding mechanisms before a core premise is shown to work (cites `abo_frozen_lessons_learned`). Defers the exact mathematical form of the margin loss to E10. Status "Complete," no hedging on the simplification decision itself (it's a design decision, not an empirical claim). No bug-fix mention. Cross-reference: explicitly builds on and formally supersedes the density-inclusive design in PHASE_E5_ALGORITHM_DESIGN.md, citing PHASE_E6 and PHASE_E7 as justification; hands off to PHASE_E9, E10, E11.

## PHASE_E9_TRAINABLE_BOUNDARY_HEAD.md

Math spec replacing the offline sklearn logistic-regression boundary classifier (used throughout E1.3-E7) with an in-network trainable 1x1x1 Conv3d head, to eliminate staleness/feedback risk (Failure Mode 6 from E5). Notably, the document explicitly documents its own mid-design correction: the "original design" (boundary head attached directly to `dec1`, trained with plain BCE, gradients flowing back into the trunk) is stated to have been found wrong "during the E11.5 readiness review" — because it would make the boundary head a second, unbudgeted segmentation classifier competing with `seg_head` for control over the shared trunk. A bare no-supervision alternative ("Option B") was also explicitly rejected as insufficiently constrained/interpretable. The corrected design detaches `dec1` before feeding the boundary head (`dec1.detach()`), so the head stays supervised/interpretable via BCE but contributes zero gradient back to the shared trunk — only entering training indirectly via the (also-detached) B_i weight used in the margin loss. This is an explicit, well-documented instance of a caught-and-fixed design bug prior to implementation, discovered by a subsequent review (E11.5) and retrofitted into this document rather than silently corrected. Flags an explicit open validation question: whether the jointly-trained head, once active, still measures "the same thing" the offline E1.3 classifier measured — deferred to a required E12 pilot-phase sanity check, not assumed to hold. Cross-reference: explicitly says it replaces the method in PHASE_E1_3_LATENT_GEOMETRY.md, plugs into PHASE_E8_EGGO_V1_SIMPLIFIED.md, and states the correction affects PHASE_E11_IMPLEMENTATION_SPEC.md's gradient table (should be "updated alongside this correction") — an explicit note that another document needs revision because of this one.

## PHASE_E5_LOSS_MATH_LITERATURE.md

Narrow technical literature search (follow-up to E2) hunting specifically for mathematical loss forms to build a two-term (separation + compactification), uncertainty-gated geometry loss. Identifies two decisive precedents: De Brabandere et al. (CVPR 2017 workshop, discriminative instance-segmentation loss) for the additive two-term variance/distance structure, and An et al.'s DyCON (CVPR 2025) for uncertainty-gating a contrastive term via voxel-wise entropy — analogous to gating by this project's evidential head instead of softmax entropy. States plainly "no single paper combines both properties," positioning the planned design as a genuine combination, not a reproduction. Also reports a tractability finding: full pairwise/graph Laplacian regularization is confirmed impractical at this project's scale (O(n²)), with kNN sparsification (k=20, reusing the density measurement) as the standard tractable fix. No self-contradiction, no bug-fix mention. Cross-reference: explicit follow-up to PHASE_E2_LITERATURE_REVIEW.md and directly feeds PHASE_E5_ALGORITHM_DESIGN.md (full derivation lives there).

## PHASE_E10_MARGIN_LOSS_SELECTION.md

Evaluates 5 candidate margin/metric-learning loss forms (contrastive/SupCon, triplet, hinge margin, ArcFace/CosFace angular margin, and a "plain hinge" collapsed into the same category as #3) against EGGO-v1's specific constraints, rejecting 3: SupCon (internal log-sum-exp normalization entangles push/pull, cited SINCERE paper's documented unwanted intra-class repulsion side effect), triplet (adds an avoidable sampling-strategy hyperparameter when minimizing degrees of freedom is the explicit goal), and ArcFace/CosFace (built for many-class softmax settings, ill-suited to 2-class/imbalanced dense per-voxel prediction without nontrivial unvalidated adaptation). Selects the De Brabandere-style pairwise hinge margin (already introduced in E5) as satisfying all 4 stated constraints cleanly. No self-contradiction — a clean selection process with reasoned rejections, not walked back. No bug-fix mention. Cross-reference: draws all candidate formulas from PHASE_E5_LOSS_MATH_LITERATURE.md, plugs the selected loss into PHASE_E8_EGGO_V1_SIMPLIFIED.md's design, feeds PHASE_E11_IMPLEMENTATION_SPEC.md.

## PHASE_E11_5_READINESS_REVIEW.md

## PHASE_E12A_SMOKE_TEST.md

First actual code/training run for EGGO-M (2-3 epochs, seed 0). Reports two real bugs found and explicitly fixed, not hidden: (1) pre-flight discovery that the "frozen" v1 baseline (`neuroscan_3d_fixed.py`) was never actually git-tracked despite the `baseline-frozen` tag existing — the tag never protected real file content — fixed by committing it before building v2; (2) the first smoke-test attempt appeared to hang due to a Python-level per-voxel `for` loop with an implicit CPU↔GPU sync on `.tolist()` calls in `compute_margin_loss`, contradicting the vectorized computation the E11 complexity analysis assumed — rewritten as batched matrix operations, reducing margin-loss compute from effectively unbounded/hanging to 0.16s per batch. ## PHASE_E12D_MECHANISM_FAILURE_INVESTIGATION.md

Post-hoc investigation (no retraining) into why E12b's margin didn't grow, testing 4 candidate hypotheses (H1: hinge rarely active, H2: lambda too weak, H3: gate saturates near-zero, H4: margin threshold miscalibrated to feature-space scale). Decisive finding: H1, H3, and H4 are all supported and form one coherent story — the hinge threshold `2*delta_d=2.0` was set roughly an order of magnitude below the actual observed pairwise distance scale (19-34 throughout training), so the hinge was essentially never active (0.65%→0.00% by epoch 25); independently, the gate weight `B_i` collapses toward zero (97-98.6% near-zero by epoch 20-30) because `tau_b` was derived from a static, fully-converged offline checkpoint but the live boundary head's logit magnitude grows rapidly during training, making the fixed `tau_b` obsolete quickly — an issue the original E5 design spec had explicitly flagged as a risk ("must be re-derived... not a final constant") but which this document confirms is a live, significant issue, not just a hypothetical caveat. H2 (lambda too weak) is found to be a secondary, downstream consequence rather than an independent cause: gradient measurements show the margin gradient is nonzero (even briefly larger than the segmentation gradient at epoch 1) but decays because the underlying loss itself is near-zero due to H1/H4, not because lambda itself is the primary bottleneck. Explicitly and carefully distinguishes its finding from a more "interesting" alternative reading (that latent margin was never the real bottleneck for segmentation) — stating plainly "that is not what was found here." Recommends recalibrating both constants from live measured data before any lambda sweep, and explicitly says "do not yet proceed to E13." Cross-reference: directly follows up on PHASE_E12B_PILOT_RESULTS.md's null mechanism finding and revises constants originally derived in PHASE_E5_ALGORITHM_DESIGN.md §2.2/§4.1.

## PHASE_E12E_HYPERPARAMETER_CALIBRATION.md

Recalibrates `delta_d` and `tau_b` from measured data per E12d's recommendation, and explicitly documents a real bug found and fixed mid-phase: the first `delta_d` calibration attempt measured pairwise distances on a freshly-initialized model in `.eval()` mode, yielding delta_d=0.0148 — verified live and found to give exactly 0.0% active hinge, i.e. it failed. Root cause: `BatchNorm3d` behaves completely differently in eval vs train mode, and a freshly initialized model's eval-mode running statistics are meaningless (no batches yet seen) — measured a ~68x difference in `dec1` standard deviation between eval (0.0089) and train (0.6095) mode at true initialization. Corrected by measuring in `.train()` mode, yielding delta_d=3.6659 (247x larger than the flawed first attempt, 3.7x larger than the original uncalibrated 1.0). The document explicitly checks and confirms this bug did NOT invalidate E12d's own findings, since E12d always used post-epoch-1 checkpoints where the eval/train gap is under 10%, not a fresh model. Also confirms via direct measurement that a single fixed `tau_b` cannot work (implied tau_b grows ~11.8x from epoch 1 to 30), replacing it with a live EMA-based adaptive value. Verification run (5 epochs) shows the fix working: margin loss stays 500-1800x larger than E12b's decayed value at comparable points, active hinge % stays nonzero (0.70-3.33%) vs E12b's 0.00% by epoch 25. Explicitly states what remains unanswered: whether an active margin mechanism improves Dice/ECE (not tested by this short verification run), and whether active_hinge_pct's decline over 5 epochs will continue toward collapse over a longer run. Cross-reference: directly responds to and fixes constants flagged in PHASE_E12D_MECHANISM_FAILURE_INVESTIGATION.md; recommends a full pilot re-run (delivered in PHASE_E12F).

## PHASE_E13_CODE_AUDIT.md
